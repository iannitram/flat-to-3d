# Turn the Z13 into a true 3D cinema

*Research report, revised 2026-10-18 to take in my rulings after the first draft: no paywalled tools, and Spider-Man as the first trial. Live status is in the repo README, ROADMAP and issues.*

**The short answer:** yes, I can play a real share of his library in true two-camera 3D on a giant fixed screen in the Quest 2, all on the Z13 under SteamOS, all with free tools. But nobody has published a finished end-to-end setup that does it, so two pieces have to be built. The pieces that make the 3D already exist. **UEVR** is a free add-on that makes Unreal Engine games draw two views. **Geo-11** is a free add-on that makes DirectX 11 games draw two views and lay them side by side. Both have documented recipes for running under Proton ([UEVR on Linux](https://vronlinux.org/docs/games/uevrinjector/); [Geo-11](https://helixmod.blogspot.com/2022/06/announcing-new-geo-11-3d-driver.html)). The streaming piece also exists: **WiVRn** is a free Linux program that sends the picture to the Quest 2 over the USB cable ([WiVRn](https://github.com/WiVRn/WiVRn)). What is missing is the "giant fixed 3D TV": the bit that takes the game's two views and pins them to a still screen, so turning your head does not move the camera. No ready-made Linux tool does that. It is the first thing we build. Of the 10 Favorites, **ACE COMBAT 7, Control, Planet of Lana and STAR WARS Zero Company have a free path now**. **Spider-Man 2, Alan Wake 2 and 007 First Light** need us to build. Spider-Man 2 has the strongest head start: an open-source VR mod for its predecessor already contains the exact 3D-TV mode (covered below), and 007 has a dormant stereo mode in its own exe. **Forza Horizon 6 is "not safe"**, and **Hades and Hades II won't work**. In the wider library, 48 of the 98 scanned games have a real 3D world. Most of those are DirectX 11 Unity games, so Geo-11 is the single most valuable tool to prove first. None of this has been tested on the Z13 yet. Every "likely" below is a claim that one specific test will settle.

## Three building blocks, and only one is missing

Every working setup has three layers. The first **makes two camera views** (the "stereo source"). The second **places them on a fixed screen**. The third **carries the picture down the cable** to the headset. For my rules, the first and third layers exist as free software. The middle layer does not.

### Making two views: the free tools and what they cover

**UEVR** (praydog's Universal Unreal Engine VR mod) is the strongest free option. It covers Unreal Engine 4.8 through 5.4. Its two good modes both draw the scene twice from two real camera positions. "Native Stereo" uses Unreal's own stereo code. "Synchronized Sequential" freezes game time while it draws the second eye. A third mode, AFR, alternates eyes while the game keeps moving, and the README itself warns it causes eye desync ([UEVR](https://github.com/praydog/UEVR)). The latest stable release is 1.05 from November 2024, and nightly builds continued to 30 August 2026 ([UEVR-nightly](https://github.com/praydog/UEVR-nightly/releases)). The Linux recipe is documented, in four steps. First, install Microsoft's .NET 6 runtime into the game's private Windows folder (its "Proton prefix") with protontricks. Second, add a Steam launch option, `PROTON_REMOTE_DEBUG_CMD`, so the UEVR injector starts inside the same prefix as the game. Third, choose OpenXR when running on WiVRn. Fourth, click Inject ([VR on Linux wiki](https://vronlinux.org/docs/games/uevrinjector/)). Two known failures: the injector sometimes cannot find the game's process, and it crashes on non-English system language settings ([Proton #8346](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/Proton/issues/8346); [kaon #20](https://github.com/LorenDB/kaon/issues/20)).

**Geo-11** is a free "3D driver" built on **3DMigoto**. 3DMigoto is a long-running modding tool that sits in front of a DirectX 11 game as a stand-in `d3d11.dll` file and can rewrite its shaders (shaders are the small programs that draw each surface). Geo-11 draws real geometric 3D in DirectX 11 games and outputs it as side-by-side (SBS) or top-and-bottom (TAB) pictures. It needs no NVIDIA hardware and has run on AMD cards. Its author's announcement says **"Linux with Proton also works"**. DirectX 12 is "not planned" ([Helix Mod: Geo-11](https://helixmod.blogspot.com/2022/06/announcing-new-geo-11-3d-driver.html)). Geo-11 can also load the large back catalogue of community "3D fixes" written for NVIDIA's old 3D Vision. That matters for Control and Planet of Lana, which both have one.

For **Unity** games there are three routes. UUVR, the universal Unity VR mod, installs through Rai Pal, but it has been quiet since July 2025 and its current release is Mono-only ([UUVR](https://github.com/Raicuparta/uuvr)). (Mono and IL2CPP are the two ways Unity games package their code. Mono is easier to mod.) **UnityVRMod** covers both Mono and IL2CPP, from Unity 5.6 to the latest version, on DirectX 11 ([UnityVRMod](https://github.com/NewUnityModder/UnityVRMod)). Both load through **BepInEx**, a mod loader that runs under Proton with the `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="winhttp=n,b"` launch option ([BepInEx docs](https://docs.bepinex.dev/articles/advanced/proton_wine.html)). For RE Engine (Capcom) games, **REFramework** is actively maintained and gives full VR ([REFramework](https://github.com/praydog/REFramework)), but none are installed.

Three tools are excluded. VorpX is paid. SuperDepth3D and similar ReShade or vkBasalt effects build "3D" from a depth map, which is exactly the depth reprojection that ruling 3 forbids ([Depth3D](https://github.com/BlueSkyDefender/Depth3D)). Katanga, the free-looking Geo-11 headset viewer, ships only inside the paid HelixVision app ([Steam: HelixVision](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1127310/discussions/0/3726197825948436143)).

### Carrying the picture: WiVRn over the existing cable

**WiVRn** is both the headset "runtime" (the program that VR games talk to through the standard OpenXR interface) and the streamer. It is on Flathub, which suits SteamOS's locked system files. It lists Quest 2 as supported. It documents a wired mode: run `adb reverse tcp:9757 tcp:9757`, then open the headset app with a `wivrn+tcp://localhost` link ([WiVRn](https://github.com/WiVRn/WiVRn)). On AMD it encodes video with the GPU's built-in H.265 video engine, so encoding does not eat into the game's graphics budget. A Half-FPS mode lets a game run at half the headset's refresh rate, which fits the 30+ fps target ([WiVRn releases](https://github.com/WiVRn/WiVRn/releases)). The Flatpak bundles **xrizer**, a translator that lets older OpenVR-style mods talk to WiVRn. Games opt in with `PRESSURE_VESSEL_IMPORT_OPENXR_1_RUNTIMES=1 %command%` ([vronlinux: xrizer](https://vronlinux.org/docs/fossvr/xrizer/)).

This fits Quest Tether with very little change. `adb reverse` can hold several port mappings at once. Quest Tether's gnirehtet relay on port 31416 can keep giving the headset internet, while one more mapping carries WiVRn directly over the USB tunnel without passing through gnirehtet. That matters because the ~28 Mbit/s seen with Steam Link has not been tested as gnirehtet's real ceiling. WiVRn v26.9 also added a "native USB mode" for Meta headsets that needs no adb or developer mode. The release notes do not say whether Quest 2 is included ([WiVRn v26.9](https://github.com/WiVRn/WiVRn/releases/tag/v26.9)).

**Steam Link VR** has worked from Linux hosts since late 2025, and SteamVR 2.17 (September 2026) added "initial support for USB streaming". Neither report says whether that USB support covers the Quest or only Valve's own Steam Frame, and the press still calls Linux SteamVR "in quite a rough spot" ([GamingOnLinux, Sep 2026](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/09/steamvr-2-17-arrives-ready-to-go-for-the-steam-frame/); [GamingOnLinux, Apr 2026](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/04/steamvr-beta-2-16-3-brings-linux-fixes-for-vrlink/)). It is the fallback: I already uses the app, but it is closed source and harder to control. **ALVR** is out: a Bazzite user reports it looping between connect and disconnect on a Quest 2, wired or not ([ALVR #3034](https://github.com/alvr-org/ALVR/issues/3034)). One practical point: the flat Steam Link app and WiVRn both want the headset's foreground. The future "Start 3D" button has to switch between them.

### Pinning it to a fixed screen: what we must build

The notes found **no Linux tool that shows a live 3D game as a still, per-eye giant screen** on WiVRn. Three near-misses came up. WayVR shows desktop windows in VR but has no 3D mode ([WayVR](https://github.com/wayvr-org/wayvr)). vr-video-player does show side-by-side windows in 3D, but only on SteamVR and X11 ([vr-video-player](https://git.dec05eba.com/vr-video-player/about/)). **VRto3D** does the reverse job. It is a SteamVR driver with a fixed, head-tracking-off virtual headset, and it turns a VR mod's two eyes into a flat SBS picture for 3D monitors. It runs natively on Linux ([VRto3D](https://github.com/oneup03/VRto3D)).

The clean answer uses a feature built into OpenXR. A "quad layer" is a flat rectangle placed in the room at a fixed spot, and each quad carries an `eyeVisibility` setting that can make it visible to the left eye only or the right eye only ([Khronos OpenXR spec](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenXR/specs/1.1/man/html/XrCompositionLayerQuad.html)). Put the left view on a left-only quad and the right view on a right-only quad, both at the same spot 3 m away and 4 m wide, and you have a true 3D TV hanging in space. The headset's own compositor keeps that screen rock-steady even when the game only manages 30 fps. Two small programs follow from this:

**Build #1, the fixed-screen shim.** In OpenXR terms this is an "API layer": a plug-in that sits between a VR mod and WiVRn. It tells the game the head is always still, so the camera never follows my head. Then it re-presents the game's two views as the two quads. It works for any mod that talks OpenXR: UEVR and the Unity VR mods. Its other job is to ask the game for TV-sized images (about 1280×720 per eye) rather than full headset resolution, which saves GPU time.

**Build #2, the SBS viewer.** This is a tiny WiVRn app. It captures a side-by-side game window (through PipeWire, Linux's screen-capture system), cuts it in half, and shows the halves as the two quads. It serves Geo-11, VRto3D and any future stereo layer of ours. vr-video-player proves the idea. The port is small.

Both builds rest on one assumption that must be checked first: that WiVRn honours `eyeVisibility`. If it ignores it, both eyes see both quads and the 3D collapses.

## Where the notes disagree, and what settles it

The five notes contradict each other in five places. Each one changes a verdict, so here is how each was resolved.

**Are R.E.A.L. VR mods usable?** The notes disagreed on whether they are free (March 2026 reports of a 38-game free tier including Spider-Man 2) or Patreon-only ($10/month, with 40+ mods pulled after DMCA requests in January 2026) ([Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/luke-ross-vr-mods-free-cyberpunk-2077/); [Mixed News](https://mixed-news.com/en/real-vr-mod-dlss-ray-reconstruction/)). **I settled it: no paywall-guarded tools, even ones he already owns** (DECISIONS ruling 16). R.E.A.L. VR is out, and so is NoMoreFlat until a genuinely free build exists. The same ruling removed this report's first Spider-Man 2 route. Its replacement, SpidyVR, is better on every count: open source, both eyes in the same frame rather than alternating, and already a 3D-screen mode.

**Does UEVR's "2D Screen Mode" keep the 3D?** A plugin author who builds on that mode says each eye keeps its own view, "so the quad has depth", and that head movement is not applied to the camera ([uevr-cutscene-comfort](https://github.com/LorenDB/uevr-cutscene-comfort)). A January 2026 settings guide says the mode "disables stereoscopic depth" ([vrpupu](https://vrpupu.com/en/2026/01/uevr-advanced-settings-explained/)). The plugin author's account is more technical and first-hand, and it also describes a separate "flat 2D" variant that gives both eyes the same picture. Those two settings may be what the guide confused. If the author is right, **every Unreal game gets the giant 3D TV with no build at all**, and Build #1 becomes a fallback for the other engines. The test takes five minutes. In UEVR on the Z13, toggle 2D Screen Mode and close one eye, then the other. If near objects jump sideways between eyes, the depth is kept.

**Does Geo-11 work under Proton on AMD?** Geo-11's author says yes, in one line ([Helix Mod](https://helixmod.blogspot.com/2022/06/announcing-new-geo-11-3d-driver.html)). There is also a second-hand report of a Geo-11 fix that crashed on Windows 10 but ran on a Steam Deck. The platform note found no Linux evidence of its own and called 3DMigoto/Geo-11 "a poor bet". Its reasoning is that the stand-in `d3d11.dll` has to pass calls on to DXVK, Proton's translator from DirectX 11 to Vulkan, and nobody documents that working. This report sides with the author's claim but treats it as unproven. It is the **highest-value single test** in the project, because about 33 library games ride on it. The test: put Geo-11 into Control's DX11 build with `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="d3d11,nvapi64=n,b"` and look at the Z13's own screen for a side-by-side picture with visible parallax.

**Does AER count as "true stereo"?** Alternate Eye Rendering (AER) is used by the R.E.A.L. VR author and by NoMoreFlat. It renders a real camera view for one eye per frame and shows the other eye's previous image ([the R.E.A.L. VR author](https://www.patreon.com/posts/lets-do-space-74012973)). The depth comes from two real camera positions, not from a depth map, so it passes ruling 3 as written. But each eye refreshes at half the game's frame rate, and fast sideways camera moves show ghosting or "eye-flips". The tools note would rule out UEVR's equivalent AFR mode on the same grounds. This report treats AER as **allowed but second-tier**: acceptable where nothing better exists, never chosen over a same-moment two-view mode. Since the paywall ruling removed both AER mods, the question now matters only for our own builds, where AER is the fallback if a heavy game can't afford two views per frame. **I has not ruled on it yet.**

**Is Forza Horizon 6 "build" or "not safe"?** The library note lists it as "build" because, after a first-launch sign-in, it plays offline on a Steam Deck ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/05/forza-horizon-6-is-out-valve-update-proton-hotfix-for-linux-initial-thoughts/comment_id=294767)). The games note lists it as not safe. Its reasons: PCGamingWiki says "Broadband Internet connection required", the game runs a shared online world, saves sync to the Xbox account, and my own game notes already forbids injected DLLs in this game ([PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Forza_Horizon_6); my notes on Forza Horizon 6). my existing rule wins: **not safe**. The only known stereo mod is paid on Ko-fi and unfinished in any case ([MIXED](https://mixed-news.com/en/watch-forza-horizon-6-in-vr-with-a-motion-simulator-looks-absolutely-insane/)).

**ACE COMBAT 7** is the best candidate. It is Unreal 4 on DX11, Denuvo was removed in 2021, it has no anti-cheat, and its campaign is offline. PCGamingWiki's 3D row says to use UEVR with VRto3D and import the community compatibility profile ([PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Ace_Combat_7:_Skies_Unknown)). A December 2025 player finished the whole campaign in UEVR "with few problems". The official VR cockpits for four planes work flawlessly, but other planes lose some radar readouts ([r/acecombat](https://redlib.hackliberty.org/r/acecombat/comments/1pij7dz/ac7_in_vr_kinda_rips/ntdw4rw/?context=3)). AC7 already runs at a 90 fps base on the Z13 (my game notes), so two views at 30–45 fps is a comfortable estimate. One caveat: the compatibility mod's Nexus page returned an error to the researchers, so its price is unconfirmed. Nexus mods are usually free. It has to be mounted from `sandisk_4tb` before it can be the first test.

**Control** has a free route that draws both eyes at the same moment, which is better than AER. Losti's 3D fix is DX11-only and has known flaws: some shadows wrong, reflections imperfect (turn global reflections off), HUD depth glitches. Commenters in 2025 report it working with Geo-11 ([Helix Mod: Control fix](https://helixmod.blogspot.com/2019/10/control-3d-vision-ready-fix-losti-v100.html)). Run `Control_DX11.exe` with `-dx11`. **First remove the HelixSR/OptiScaler test files** now in the game folder, using their own `HelixSR-Scaler-remove.sh`. Two stand-in graphics DLLs in one folder is a known cause of conflicts (local inspection of the install). The estimate is roughly 30–40 fps in 3D from today's 60 fps base, with frame generation off.

**Planet of Lana** has a dedicated Geo-11 fix from December 2025. It corrects decals, water reflections and caustics per eye, which only makes sense because the scenes are real 3D sets ([Helix Mod: Lana fix](https://helixmod.blogspot.com/2025/12/planet-of-lana-geo-11-fix.html)). Expect a layered diorama look rather than deep space. Frame rate is not a worry. One risk to test is whether the fix's bundled `nvapi64.dll` clashes with Proton's own handling on an AMD GPU.

**STAR WARS Zero Company** is a turn-based tactics game with an orbiting overhead camera, so 3D would give a tabletop look, and frame rate barely matters. It is Unreal 5.6, which is past UEVR's documented 4.8–5.4 range, and no one has reported trying it ([PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Star_Wars_Zero_Company)). There is useful local evidence: the game ships its debug-symbol file `SWZeroCompany.pdb`, and has no anti-cheat files (local inspection). If the UEVR nightly fails, that symbol file makes a custom fix far easier, because it names the engine's internal functions. The EA account link is a one-time prompt, not always-online play.

**Marvel's Spider-Man 2** builds on **SpidyVR** ([GitHub](https://github.com/IlyaMez/SpidyVR)), a free GPL-3.0 VR mod for Spider-Man Remastered, updated almost daily (v0.3.8, 2026-10-17). Spidy makes the game draw both eyes in the same frame. It already has the giant-3D-TV mode: **VR VIEW: GAME SCREEN** with **GAME SCREEN 3D** plays the game on a big 3D screen with the controller, the head not steering the camera. Its Linux guide reports runs under Proton with SteamVR and with WiVRn over a USB cable. `Spider-Man2.exe` contains the same engine system names Spidy hooks in Remastered (`PlayerModelHudFollower`, `TimeScaleSystem`, `HeroOfferProcessor`; local check), so the design carries over. The code addresses don't: Spidy reads one exact Remastered build at fixed places, so the port means re-finding roughly 90 of them for the 3D-screen path alone. That's weeks of reverse-engineering, sped up by diffing the two exes in Ghidra. PSN sign-in has been optional since January 2025, and there is no anti-cheat ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/01/playstation-network-requirement-on-pc-will-now-be-optional-with-in-game-rewards/page=1/)). I chose it as the first trial.

**Alan Wake 2** has a documented 3D route: NoMoreFlat OpenXR mod plus VRto3D ([Helix Mod: AW2](https://helixmod.blogspot.com/2025/04/alan-wake-2-nomoreflat-vrto3d.html); [PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Alan_Wake_II)). In January 2026 the modder said all their mods would go public, naming Alan Wake 2 ([FRVR](https://frvr.com/blog/news/starfield-and-stalker-2-vr-mods-made-free-for-all-following-dmcas-to-other-vr-modders)). But the modder's GitHub has no Alan Wake 2 repository, and the guide still points to Patreon ([github.com/mutars](https://github.com/mutars?tab=repositories)). Until a free download turns up, it counts as paid. The game also only manages 57–62 fps in flat mode on the Z13, so AER would give each eye about 28 fps with no headroom. And my game notes says not to mod it while the rainbow-foliage bug work needs a clean install (my notes on Alan Wake 2). Verdict: **build, later**. the modder's open-source, MIT-licensed `starfield2vr` and `anvilengine2vr` are the templates for that work ([starfield2vr](https://github.com/mutars/starfield2vr)).

**007 First Light** is the surprise. Denuvo was removed on 2026-07-24 ([Hardware Busters](https://hwbusters.com/news/007-first-light-drops-denuvo-after-two-months-and-the-patch-notes-never-mentioned-it/)), and there is no anti-cheat. A local read of the game's exe finds a dormant VR mode and a separate flat **"Stereoscopic" display option with depth and strength settings**. The internal names include `UI_OPTION_DISPLAY_STEREOSCOPIC`, `SetVrModeActive` and `HMD not found.`, and `openvr_api.dll` sits beside the exe (local inspection of `Retail/007FirstLight.exe`). This code is almost certainly inherited from Hitman 3, which shares the Glacier 2 engine and gained PC VR in 2022 ([Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/hitman-3-pc-vr-support-release/)). If it can be switched on, it is the game's **own two-camera renderer**, the best kind of 3D. The cheap test: start SteamVR with VRto3D's virtual headset running, then launch the game. "HMD not found" suggests it looks for a headset at startup. If that fails, the next step is the community's Glacier modding tools, and after that a custom DX12 hook.

**Hades and Hades II** stay "won't work". A Supergiant developer confirmed Hades uses pre-rendered sprite frames, not real-time 3D ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1145360/discussions/0/1738883810796606862)). Hades II does draw real 3D characters: its install ships Granny 3D models and 3D shaders (local inspection). But they stand on flat painted floors under a fixed top-down camera. Two cameras would make cardboard cut-outs over a flat backdrop, which does not justify a DX12 build.

## The library: 48 real-3D games, and DirectX 11 carries most of them

Every install folder was scanned read-only for engine signatures, version strings and the graphics library each exe loads. **No game contains EasyAntiCheat or BattlEye files** (local scan of `library-scan.tsv` paths). Eleven games have a native Linux build installed. Eight of them are 2D. Two of the rest, Core Keeper and Geometry Wars 3, must be switched to the Windows version in Steam's Compatibility setting before any Windows add-on will load. (Tiny Glade is also native, but it is in the build bucket anyway.) 32-bit games (DREDGE, Stikbold!) need the 32-bit versions of the tools.

These counts differ from the library note's mechanical tally (5 / 35 / 7 / 1 / 50) in one place: Forza Horizon 6 moved from build to not safe, per the reconciliation above. ("DX9" is DirectX 9, an older graphics version. **dgVoodoo2** translates it to DX11 so Geo-11 can work on it.) Several "likely" games will give little depth even if they work, because they are top-down or nearly flat: Mini Motorways, Core Keeper, DAVE THE DIVER, Sektori and Passant. Games with online modes (Golf Gang, V Rising, GUNTOUCHABLES) are "likely" for offline or solo play only. The scan also corrected two of my game notes. **Garden Life is Unreal Engine 5, not Unity**, and **TMNT: Splintered Fate is a custom engine, not Unity**. Five games have no per-game notes yet: Construction Simulator, Keylocker, Ereban: Shadow Legacy, CraftCraft and Baldur's Gate II EE.

The library is DirectX 11-heavy and Unity-heavy. So **proving Geo-11 under Proton on one Unity game unlocks about 25 titles at once**, more than every other step combined. UEVR covers the 7 Unreal games including AC7. The DX12 bucket is small in the library but holds most of the Favorites in trouble: four of the build/not-safe Favorites are DX12, plus Zero Company if UEVR fails.

The scan also yields a **file-based detection recipe** the app can reuse:

| Engine | How to spot it in the game folder |
|---|---|
| Unity | `UnityPlayer.dll` plus a `_Data` folder. `GameAssembly.dll` means IL2CPP; a `Managed` folder means Mono. The version string is near the top of `globalgamemanagers`. |
| Unreal | An `Engine/` folder and a `-Win64-Shipping.exe`. UE4 versions read from a `++UE4+Release-4.xx` string. |
| 2D engines | `data.win` = GameMaker; `.pck` = Godot; `FNA.dll` = FNA; `love.dll` = LÖVE; `renpy/` = Ren'Py. |
| Graphics version | Read from the DLLs the exe imports. |

## A four-phase plan from first test to our own DX12 3D

### Phase 1: prove the pipeline on games that should already work

I picked Spider-Man as the first trial, so Phase 1 opens with **SpidyVR on Spider-Man Remastered** in GAME SCREEN 3D through WiVRn on USB. Spidy already includes its own fixed 3D screen, so this one run proves the streamer, the cable, Proton injection, the 8060S's frame rate and the 3D-TV experience together. Next, UEVR on an installed UE4 game. Sifu or Little Nightmares EE are on the Z13 now. Ace Combat 7 follows once `sandisk_4tb` is mounted. This first run uses UEVR's normal head-tracked VR on purpose. It tests the streamer, the cable, the Proton injection and the 8060S's frame rate, before anything gets built. The same session settles the 2D Screen Mode question. In parallel, test Geo-11 on Control (DX11) and Planet of Lana, judged only by whether a side-by-side picture appears on the Z13's own screen. No headset is needed for that check. At the end of Phase 1 we know whether the UE bucket needs Build #1 at all, and whether the 33-game Geo-11 bucket is real.

### Phase 2: build the giant fixed 3D TV

Build #2, the SBS viewer, comes first if Geo-11 works, because it serves the largest number of games and is the smaller program. Build #1, the fixed-screen shim, comes first if Geo-11 fails or UEVR's 2D Screen Mode turns out flat. Both start with the `eyeVisibility` check on WiVRn. Pick the screen size and distance once, so every game appears on the same TV. Then confirm VRto3D's role: it is a fallback route for mods documented against it (AC7's profile), not the main path. It needs SteamVR running with its virtual headset, and the platform note warns that SteamVR cannot use VRto3D's headset and stream to the Quest at the same time.

### Phase 3: build the Inject app

**Rai Pal** is the model. It is a free (GPL-3.0) mod manager in Rust that finds games across Steam, GOG, Epic and Itch, detects engine and version, installs universal mods from a database it reads by URL, and ships a Linux AppImage. v0.24.0 came out 2026-10-17 ([Rai Pal](https://github.com/Raicuparta/rai-pal)). Our app copies its shape with three changes. A **command-line engine** does the detection (the recipe above), the installing and the launch-option writing. A **Decky plugin** replaces Rai Pal's window, so Game Mode gets an "Inject" and a "Start 3D" button. And the **recipe database is a set of `stereo-*` header keys per game (schema in DECISIONS.md), kept in this repo**, instead of a remote database. If we reuse Rai Pal's code, our app must also be GPL-3.0.

A recipe needs about a dozen keys: stereo status, method (UEVR / Geo-11 / BepInEx mod / native / build), engine and version, graphics API, mod link and version, files to copy, DLL overrides, launch options, output type (OpenXR or SBS), safe yes/no, and the date of the last Z13 test. "Inject" then becomes four steps. Copy the files. Write the Steam launch options, the way my existing frame-generation launch-option helper already does safely. Start WiVRn and the right screen program. Tell Quest Tether to add the 9757 mapping and switch the headset app. Reversibility matters as much as installing, as Control's leftover HelixSR files show. Every inject needs a matching clean "remove".

### Phase 4: our own 3D for DX12 games

The notes offer two approaches, and both have a place. The **broad approach** is a Vulkan-level stereo layer. Proton turns every DirectX version into Vulkan, so one add-on at that level could in principle work on any game. **VKS3D** (GPL-3.0, active October 2026, written with AI help) already does this on Windows. It makes the GPU draw every object twice, shifted per eye, and outputs side-by-side. It lists Linux builds, but nobody has reported them working ([VKS3D](https://github.com/ThreeDeeJay/VKS3D)). The catch is the one NVIDIA's old 3D Vision had. HUDs land at the wrong depth, and reflections, ambient shadows and other screen-space effects break until someone fixes them shader by shader. It also roughly doubles the drawing work, so it fits lighter games better than Alan Wake 2. The **per-game approach** is an in-game hook in the style of the modder's mods. It finds the game's camera, shifts it per eye with an off-centre projection aimed at the virtual screen plane, and alternates eyes (AER) to keep the cost flat. That suits heavy games on the 8060S, but each game is its own reverse-engineering project, taking weeks. The order: first try 007's dormant built-in mode, which may cost nothing. Then test VKS3D's Linux build as-is on one light game. Then pick one Favorite for a per-game hook (Alan Wake 2 once its bug-fix work ends, or Zero Company if UEVR fails, using its debug symbols).

## Risks, open questions and the first tests

The largest risk is that **Windows VR mods may not reach a Linux headset runtime from inside Proton**. UEVR and SpidyVR each have a documented Linux recipe, but neither has been run on this machine, on a Quest 2, or on an AMD integrated GPU. Two more risks concern frame rate. Frame generation (lsfg-vk, which I uses widely) is untested on a side-by-side picture and may smear the seam between eyes, so plan with it off. And every frame-rate figure in this report is an estimate drawn from today's flat-mode numbers. Game Mode is a further unknown: no one has reported WiVRn running alongside gamescope, SteamOS's Game Mode compositor, so Phase 1 should run in Desktop Mode. A housekeeping risk: UEVR's crash on non-English language settings and the Proton process-detection bug could make the Inject button flaky. The app needs clear error messages.

Settled since the first draft: no paywalled tools, and the first trial is Spider-Man (Spidy on Remastered, then the Spider-Man 2 port). Still open: does AER count as true stereo for our own builds (recommended: yes, as second tier)? Should FH6 stay "not safe" (recommended: yes, it matches his own wiki rule)?

## Conclusion

The project is smaller than it first looks. The hard problem, making a game draw two real views, is already solved for free in most of my 3D library, as long as two community tools behave under Proton the way their authors say. The real gap is the giant fixed screen in the middle, and OpenXR's per-eye screen layers make that a small, generic program rather than per-game work. Build it once and every mod that works on Linux turns into a 3D TV. That reorders the effort: the Inject app and the wiki recipes are bookkeeping on top of two short programs, and the DX12 work, the only open-ended part, mostly affects Favorites that already have partial leads.

The project also rests on a few cheap tests: winking at UEVR, a side-by-side picture from Control, a red and blue rectangle in the headset. Each one takes minutes and moves dozens of games between columns. The most interesting lead is 007 First Light, whose own renderer apparently still holds a stereoscopic mode. Hidden engine features like this may be worth a quick `strings` check across the rest of the DX12 library before anyone writes a custom hook. Spider-Man adds a second lesson: before writing a stereo core from scratch, look for an **open-source full-VR mod that already has a flat or 3D screen mode**, for the game or a sibling on the same engine, and reuse its stereo core.
